The same points every day

Stretches where an area has been above its level on four weekdays in five.
07:15 — 08:30
Morning handover, then breakfast. The corridor reaches 62 dB and dining 64 dB, with the supervised kitchen at 66 dB. All three are above their levels on four days in five.
12:00 — 13:00
Lunch. Dining averages 68 dB against a level of 65, every weekday without exception, and the kitchen reaches 70 dB.
15:30 — 17:30
Visiting, then the evening meal. The longest difficult stretch on the ward: the lounge reaches 71 dB at 16:00 and dining 69 dB at 17:00.
20:00 — 20:30
Evening handover. The corridor returns to 62 dB and bedroom wings A and C to 47 dB as patients settle.
After 15:00
Bedroom wing A and the main corridor record no daylight at all from mid-afternoon. Both run on artificial light for the rest of the day. The garden access corridor keeps daylight an hour longer than anywhere else indoors.

An area with no reading is shown as not reporting on the ward plan rather than as within its level, so a silent sensor is never read as a quiet room.

What the sensors record

Sound sensors measure sound pressure only. They do not record speech and hold no audio. Foot-traffic sensors count people crossing a line and hold no images. Light meters measure illuminance and the share of it that is daylight.

Readings are held for 90 days at full detail and as hourly averages after that. A patient's location history is visible only to the clinical team caring for them.
