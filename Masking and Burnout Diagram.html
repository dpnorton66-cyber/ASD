Why hidden distress goes unsupported, and how it ends in burnout

This view shows how pressure to fit in leads autistic people to mask, how masking hides need so that support does not arrive, and how sustained masking builds into autistic burnout. It also shows how visible distress can bring either support or exclusion, depending on how it is read. View 1 covers the overload engine. View 3 covers the environment in detail.

Every link has been checked against published research and given an evidence grade. Select a + or − sign to see the evidence and sources for that link. The full review is in the evidence review report.

Open questions

  1. Does low wellbeing or self-esteem feed back into masking? Internalised stigma is one reason autistic adults give for camouflaging, which would close a loop from the outcome back to masking.
  2. Should acceptance be a variable separate from accommodations? The evidence that masking falls concerns feeling accepted, not adjustments as such.
  3. Is the renumbering acceptable: B9 for the loop drawn as R2, with R2 now running through masking and stress?
  4. Should disclosure become a lever, such as support to disclose safely?
